# Performance Deep-Dive — 2026-06-15

Full solo-booted benchmark suite after the two perf fixes landed on
`rewrite/dispatcher-collapse` (scope lazy-fication `46882df` + body/scalar fast
path `d122734`, on top of the orjson fast-path restore `77a97ca`). Five
frameworks, seven endpoint shapes, conn=1 and conn=8, medians of 3, **one
server resident at a time**. Includes a drift-controlled before/after A/B for
the three endpoints the fixes touched, and a newly surfaced streaming
bottleneck.

Raw data: `benchmarks/results_2026-06-15.json` (full matrix),
plus same-session A/B JSON captured under `/tmp/ab_before.json` /
`/tmp/ab_after.json`.

---

## 0. Headline

- **fastapi-turbo now beats Go Gin on `/list` (28.1 vs 29.0 µs) and is within
  noise of Gin's pure-Rust floor on `/_ping`.** It still trails Gin on
  `/hello`, `/path`, `/headers`, `/with-deps`, and `POST /items`, and trails
  Fastify across the board.
- **The two fixes are real and confirmed by a same-session A/B**: −2.7 µs
  `/hello`, −4.9 µs `/list`, −5.3 µs `/items` at conn=1 (+15–21 % req/s), with
  even larger conn=8 throughput gains (`/list` 62k → 79k req/s). On the
  drift-robust delta-vs-own-ping metric the `/items` Python-crossing cost
  dropped from +17.6 µs to +12.4 µs.
- **New finding — streaming is the worst relative gap**: `GET /stream`
  (10-chunk async generator) is **546 µs on turbo vs 183 µs on FA+uvicorn** —
  3× *slower*. Root cause located: every `__anext__()` is driven through
  `run_until_complete` on a per-stream event loop (one full asyncio loop
  iteration per chunk). Concrete fix candidate below.
- **Still ~8–9× faster than stock FastAPI+uvicorn** on every request shape
  (e.g. `/hello` 25 µs vs 189 µs; `/list` 28 µs vs 267 µs).

### Streaming (Python-only, turbo vs uvicorn)

`/stream` (10-chunk async generator) is implemented only in the Python
`_bench_app`; the gin/fastify/axum mirrors don't have it, so this is a turbo-vs-
uvicorn apples-to-apples row.

| endpoint | turbo (pre-fix) | **turbo (c7113ee)** | FA+uvicorn |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `GET /stream` conn=1 p50 | 546 µs | **93 µs** | 183 µs |

**UPDATE (c7113ee): bottleneck #1 FIXED — `/stream` 546 → 93 µs (5.9×), now ~2× faster
than uvicorn.** Root cause was the per-chunk `run_until_complete`; the fix drives the
whole async generator with ONE `run_until_complete` over a Python driver coroutine
(`responses._drive_stream`) that pushes each chunk to the Rust mpsc via a `#[pyclass]
ChunkPush` callback (N loop-iterations → 1). The naive per-chunk `send(None)` probe
(suggested below) was NOT used — it's documented-unsafe (`streaming.rs:74-82`: a probe
that suspends on real I/O leaves the async-gen unrecoverable). Two bugs fixed en route:
`aclose()` only on the disconnect-break path (it hangs on an already-finished
threadpool-wrapped gen), and an `Option<Sender>` take to break a GC-cycle channel leak
when the driver raises mid-stream. A genuinely-awaiting gen (`await` between chunks) is
~278 µs — irreducible event-loop scheduling, and real token streams are await-dominated.
Verified independently: solo bench 93 µs × 3 (<1 µs spread); SSE keepalive,
disconnect-cancellation, broken-session-raise, mid-stream-validation tripwires all green.

**This is the one shape where turbo is materially slower than uvicorn (3×).**
Body verified correct (200, 80 bytes, all 10 chunks). Root cause in §3.

## 2. Before / after — the two perf fixes (drift-controlled A/B)

Same-session A/B: built `77a97ca` (pre-fix `src/router.rs` +
`applications.py`), benched turbo, rebuilt `d122734` (current), benched again —
**back to back in one machine window** so the `/_ping` floor is identical
(17.9 µs before, 17.8 µs after). This is immune to the cross-session drift that
inflated §1's absolute turbo numbers (§4).

### conn=1, p50 µs (20k/3k, median of 3)

| endpoint | before | after | Δ p50 | before Δ-ping | after Δ-ping | before req/s | after req/s | req/s gain |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `/hello` | 24.5 | **21.8** | **−2.7** | +6.6 | +4.0 | 33,389 | 38,348 | **+15 %** |
| `/list`  | 28.6 | **23.7** | **−4.9** | +10.7 | +5.9 | 28,711 | 34,740 | **+21 %** |
| `POST /items` | 35.5 | **30.2** | **−5.3** | +17.6 | +12.4 | 23,675 | 28,038 | **+18 %** |

### conn=8, p50 µs / req/s

| endpoint | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| `/hello` | 89 µs / 78k | **71 µs / 94k** |
| `/list`  | 115 µs / 62k | **89 µs / 79k** |
| `POST /items` | 122 µs / 58k | **109 µs / 64k** |

**Attribution.** Fix 1 (skip `set_request_scope_ctxvar` when no exception
handler / Sentry consumer) removed a ~4–5 µs PyO3→Python call on every Python
request — the dominant win, visible on all three rows. Fix 2 (lazy
`query_multi` + content-type carrier so body-only routes skip the `HeaderMap`
clone and the redundant 2nd query parse) adds on top, most visible on `/items`
(the −5.3 µs total exceeds Fix 1 alone) and in conn=8 throughput where the
removed per-request allocations compound. The drop in the delta-vs-ping column
(`/items` +17.6 → +12.4) is the cleanest measure: **5.2 µs of pure
Python-crossing cost eliminated** on the body path, drift removed.

These match the implement-phase report's claims (it quoted −3–4 µs `/hello`,
−2–3 µs `/list`, −6 µs `/items` from a different machine window). The variance
between that report and §1's matrix is entirely the floor drift, not a
regression — the A/B confirms the fixes are intact.

Projected conn=1 after #1+#2 (drift-corrected A/B baseline): `/items` ≈ 27 µs
(closing on Gin's 25.5), `/stream` ≈ 250 µs (from 546). The GET-shape gap to
Gin (~4–7 µs Python-crossing) is largely the GIL round-trip + extraction floor
and is hard to close further without leaving Python on the hot path.

### Where turbo stands vs the targets

- **vs Go Gin**: turbo **wins** `/list` (28.1 vs 29.0) and ties the Rust floor;
  **loses** `/hello` (+1.7), `/path` (+3.7), `/headers` (+2.5), `/with-deps`
  (+5.9), `/items` (+6.5). Fix #2 closes most of the `/items` gap; the GET gaps
  are the GIL floor.
- **vs Fastify 5**: turbo trails on every shape (`/hello` +2.9, `/list` +6.8,
  `/items` +4.8). Fastify's V8 JIT + schema-compiled JSON serialization is the
  ceiling for a managed-runtime framework; closing it needs bottlenecks #1/#2
  plus shaving the GIL floor.

## 4. Methodology & variance

- **Machine**: Apple M-series, macOS (Darwin 25.x), loopback, *not* an isolated
  box. **This session ran ~2–4 µs hotter than the 2026-06-10 session**: every
  framework's `/_ping` floor drifted up in lockstep (raw Axum 14.8 → 16.7,
  Fastify 18.2 → 22.3, Gin 21.8 → 23.0, turbo 16.0 → 20.5). Absolute
  cross-session comparison is therefore invalid; **delta-vs-own-ping (§1) and
  the same-session A/B (§2) are the metrics to trust.** Within this session,
  turbo conn=1 per-run p50 spread was < 0.5 µs on every endpoint
  (`/items` runs: 32.0/32.0/31.5; `/hello`: 25.1/25.2/25.0).
- **Client**: `target/release/fastapi-turbo-bench` — raw TCP, keep-alive, one
  thread per connection, client-side histogram, `--format json` parsed for
  exact percentiles. (httpx/ASGITransport remains invalid for absolute numbers
  per the earlier audit.)
- **Protocol**: every cell = median of 3 independent solo-booted runs; 20k/3k
  at conn=1, 40k/4k at conn=8. **Servers booted strictly one at a time** (boot
  → wait-http → 0.5 s settle → bench all endpoints → kill → 0.8 s port-release
  → next server) via `/tmp/bench_runner.py`. The +3–5 µs co-resident inflation
  warning was respected — no two servers were ever resident together.
- **Before/after A/B**: built `77a97ca`, benched, rebuilt `d122734`, benched,
  back-to-back; `/_ping` floor 17.9 vs 17.8 confirms no intra-session drift
  between the two builds.
- **Configuration**: turbo `app.run(host, port, workers=1)`; uvicorn
  `--workers 1 --no-access-log`. FA+uvicorn run under `comparison/fastapi-venv`
  (FastAPI 0.135.3, uvicorn 0.44.0).
- **Versions**: Python 3.14.4 (GIL build) · go 1.26.2 / gin · node 25.9.0 /
  fastify 5 · raw Axum 0.8 mirror.
- **Endpoint parity**: identical `_bench_app.py` source runs under both turbo
  and uvicorn; gin/fastify/axum mirrors follow the same contract
  (`benchmarks/go-gin/main.go`, `benchmarks/fastify/server.js`,
  `comparison/rust-axum/main.rs`). `/_ping` returns identical static JSON from
  all servers that expose it.
